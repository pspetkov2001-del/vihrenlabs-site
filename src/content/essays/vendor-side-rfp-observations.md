I've watched a lot of RFPs from the vendor and distribution side.

Here's what the vendor side does when an RFP lands:

**Step 1: Assess whether you're a real competitive opportunity or a check-the-box exercise.**

Vendors know when they're invited to an RFP to fulfill a procurement policy requirement while the incumbent is being renewed. The signals are: short timeline, evaluation criteria that map perfectly to the incumbent's strengths, no preliminary conversations with the new vendors before the RFP drops.

If you want a real competitive response, you need to signal that the process is real.

**Step 2: Calculate the deal registration angle.**

Most enterprise software vendors have deal registration programs that protect the margin of the reseller who "registers" an opportunity first. If your incumbent's reseller has already registered this deal, a new reseller entering the picture creates a channel conflict the vendor has to manage — which slows down pricing approvals and limits how aggressive the new reseller can get.

Understanding whether a deal is already registered, and with whom, is procurement information that most buyers never ask about.

**Step 3: Determine the relationship position.**

Vendors track executive relationships. If the CIO at your organization has a relationship with the vendor's regional VP, that factors into how much executive attention the RFP gets and how much exception pricing gets authorized.

**What this means for your RFP:**

1. Make the competitive threat credible — demonstrate you've had substantive conversations with alternatives before the formal process starts
2. Ask about deal registration status directly when briefing resellers
3. Use the RFP to reset the relationship dynamic — the incumbent has incumbency, not a monopoly on your attention
4. Set an evaluation timeline that creates urgency without creating desperation (6-8 weeks is a credible window; 2 weeks signals you've already decided)

An RFP that produces a 5% price reduction and the same terms is not a successful RFP. An RFP that resets pricing, improves contract terms, and creates genuine relationship awareness on the vendor side is worth running.
