I've watched a lot of enterprise IT renewals from the vendor/distribution side.

The same mistakes repeat.

**Mistake 1: The renewal conversation starts at T-30.**

At 30 days before renewal, you're a retention problem. The vendor already knows your likelihood of switching — based on adoption data, support ticket volume, and whatever the account manager has heard in quarterly reviews.

If you're starting the negotiation at T-30, you're negotiating on their timeline, with their pricing logic, against your own urgency.

The leverage window is T-90. Not earlier (too theoretical), not later (too late). Open the conversation at T-90 with a clear signal that you're evaluating alternatives. Even if you aren't.

**Mistake 2: Negotiating on price instead of contract terms.**

A 10% price discount sounds like a win. Over a 3-year contract, it might be worth €15,000. An uncapped auto-renewal escalation clause at 8% per year on the same contract costs you €40,000+ over the same period.

The real value in a renewal negotiation is in the clauses, not the price. Audit rights. True-up mechanics. Auto-renewal notice window. Price escalation cap. These are worth more than the headline discount, and most IT procurement managers spend all their energy on the discount.

**Mistake 3: Not knowing what the distribution layer knows.**

If your vendor sells through a distributor or reseller, there's information in that channel that never makes it to your procurement conversation unprompted. Floor pricing. MDF availability. Rebate stacks that can be directed toward your account.

You won't get this information unless you ask for it, specifically, from someone with enough relationship to surface it.

The organizations with the best IT procurement outcomes aren't the ones with the biggest legal teams. They're the ones who start early, negotiate terms not just price, and ask the right questions of the right people in the channel.
