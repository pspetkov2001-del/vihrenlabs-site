DORA has been in force since January 17, 2025.

Most financial entities in scope have done some version of an ICT third-party register. Many believe they're compliant.

Here's the gap I see most often:

**The register covers the contracts. It doesn't cover the concentrations.**

Article 28 of DORA requires a register of information about all ICT third-party service providers. Most organizations have built this — vendor name, service category, criticality assessment, contractual references.

What's usually missing: the concentration risk picture.

DORA is explicit about systemic concentration risk. Article 28(5): competent authorities shall assess the risk posed by ICT concentration at the level of individual financial entities and at sector level. The EBA, ESMA, and EIOPA have been clear that they will look at this.

Concentration risk means: how much of your critical ICT function runs on one provider? What happens if Azure, or AWS, or your core banking vendor has a major outage? Do you have a documented exit strategy? Is the data portability clause in your contract actually usable?

The organizations that will do well in supervisory assessments have answered these questions in their register. The ones that are exposed have a vendor list, not a risk picture.

**The three concentration questions your register should answer:**

1. What percentage of your total ICT spend goes to your top 3 providers? (EBA considers >30% to a single provider a flag)
2. For each critical provider, is there a documented exit plan — not just a contractual provision, but an operational plan someone has actually reviewed?
3. Are your data portability and access clauses in your contracts written in a way that would actually work in a 90-day exit scenario?

If you can't answer all three from your current register, it's not complete enough for a supervisory assessment.
