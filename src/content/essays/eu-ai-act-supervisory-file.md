The EU AI Act GPAI enforcement starts August 2, 2026.

Most SME compliance conversations have focused on whether you're "covered" — whether your AI use cases trigger obligations or not.

That's the wrong question to start with.

The right question is: if a supervisory authority asked you to demonstrate reasonable due diligence on your AI deployments, what would you show them?

**The four things that constitute reasonable due diligence for an AI deployer:**

1. **An inventory of your AI-adjacent tools** — not just the big ones (ChatGPT, Copilot), but the AI features embedded in your CRM, your HR platform, your customer service tooling, your underwriting or credit systems. Most organizations have 5-15 AI-adjacent touchpoints they haven't formally catalogued.

2. **A risk classification for each tool** — is it in an Article 5 prohibited category? Does it touch an Annex III high-risk use case? Does it generate output that reaches customers or employees without human review? You don't need a legal opinion for every tool. You need a documented rationale for why you classified each one as you did.

3. **Article 50 transparency language in place** — for any AI system that interacts with individuals (chatbots, automated content, AI-assisted decisions communicated externally), you need disclosure language. It doesn't need to be complex. It needs to exist and be visible.

4. **A named person who owns this** — not "IT" or "Legal." One person, one email address, one accountability. Supervisory authorities follow up. You need a contact.

None of this requires an external consultant. It requires a structured inventory, 2-3 hours of classification work, and documentation that you did it.

The organizations that have this file ready are not necessarily fully compliant with every article of the regulation. But they can demonstrate they took it seriously — which is the threshold that separates "we're working on it" from "we had no idea."
